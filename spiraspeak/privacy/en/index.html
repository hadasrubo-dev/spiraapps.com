SpiraSpeak Privacy Policy

SpiraApps · עברית · Terms of use

Last updated: November 18, 2026

If the Hebrew and English versions differ, the Hebrew version prevails for users in Israel.

SpiraSpeak is an app for practising speech and voice, for iPhone and Android, currently in a trial stage. Its screens are in Hebrew, so setting names below are given in Hebrew. This page says plainly what stays on the phone, what leaves it, where it goes and why. There is no user account, no advertising and no tracking across apps or sites, and we don't sell data.

Know this up front: some features send text or sound to other companies' services (Microsoft, Apple, Google, Groq), and sharing with a therapist stores practice data on our server. The details follow.

Health-related data

Speech practice is part of therapy, so what the app measures and keeps (loudness, pitch, rate, scores, goals, the words it recognised, the age and sex you chose to give) is information about health. We treat it as sensitive. Most of it stays on the phone; what leaves is listed below.

What stays on the phone

Practice history (numbers and a loudness trace, not the words spoken), scores, goals, the voice profile (pitch figures that help tell your voice from another), age and sex, sentences you added and recordings you saved with them, settings, conversation summaries (how long you spoke, the share of strong words and the number of words, not the words themselves), the list of screens and therapists you approved, and your therapist's phone number if you typed it. On iPhone, tapping "שיחת וואטסאפ עם המטפל" (WhatsApp call with the therapist) opens WhatsApp with that number. The recording of a practice round is temporary and deleted at the next round; a recording you saved with a sentence stays until you delete the sentence.

On iPhone this data is included in the phone's backup (iCloud or a computer), so restoring a backup brings it back. On Android cloud backup is off for the app; a phone maker's direct phone-to-phone transfer may still carry the data over.

On iPhone the app reads the calendar on the phone only, to remind you of a meeting with your therapist, and uses the camera to scan a connection code (QR) and to briefly measure the distance to your face. The measurement turns on the front camera (TrueDepth, through Apple's ARKit) for about two seconds and uses only the position of the face relative to the camera to work out the distance. The face data is used for that calculation at that moment only, on the phone: it is not kept and not sent, and no image is kept or sent. Only the resulting distance, in centimetres, is kept as a measurement setting (and, like the other measurement settings, it is sent to a watcher you approved).

In the Android trial build, the recording of the last recognised round is also kept as a temporary file in the app's private folder until the next round.

Reading aloud: Microsoft

On the iPhone the default is the Microsoft voice, but only after a one-time notice where you choose between it and the phone's own voice; until you answer, the phone's own voice reads and the text stays on the phone. On Android the default is the phone's own voice, and the Microsoft voice is a choice in Settings (after the same notice). While the Microsoft voice is in use and the phone is online, every text the app reads aloud is sent as text to Microsoft's online read-aloud service (the Edge browser's service) and comes back as sound. That includes the practice sentences, sentences you wrote or your therapist sent, the spoken feedback after a round, and the AI's replies in a conversation. Microsoft sees the text and the phone's IP address. Our use of this service is not covered by an agreement between us and Microsoft, and we don't know how long it keeps the text.

Turning it off: הגדרות (Settings) → הגדרות קול → מנוע קול → "מובנה במכשיר (Apple)" on iPhone, or "מובנה במכשיר" on Android (built into the device). The phone's own voice then does the reading and the text isn't sent. If you choose "Azure AI Speech" and enter your own key, the text goes to your own Azure account at Microsoft.

Speech recognition: Apple and Google

To know which words were said, the round's recording goes through speech recognition.

iPhone: when online, the sound is sent to Apple's speech recognition service; offline, recognition happens on the phone if the device supports it. This also applies to the continuous listening on the conversation screen, which picks up other people speaking in the room. Apple handles the sound under its own privacy policy; we never receive it.

Android: by default recognition happens on the phone, in the system's recognition engine. On phones where that engine doesn't know Hebrew, the app uses Google's Hebrew model installed on the phone and asks it to work offline; that engine belongs to Google or the phone's maker, and we can't guarantee it honours the request. Google's online recognition is used only if you turn on the switch "לאפשר את מנוע הזיהוי של Google (עשוי לשלוח את הקול לשרת)" in Settings.

The recognised words are not kept in the history on the phone.

AI conversation and word guess: Groq

In "שיחה עם AI" (AI conversation), in an AI item inside a set, and in the game "ניחוש מילים" (word guess), what you said is sent as text (not sound) to our server and from there to the AI provider Groq, which runs the model and returns an answer. In a conversation each turn sends everything said so far, plus a short description the app adds about your voice (such as loudness and rate) and the form of address. In word guess it sends the description you spoke, or the secret word, which may be a personal word you added. If you turn on "חיפוש באינטרנט בשיחה עם AI (נסיוני)" (web search in the AI conversation, experimental; off by default), parts of the conversation may become search queries at Groq. Before the first use of these features the app shows a notice that explains this and asks whether to go on.

Our server passes the request on and does not store the conversation; it records only how much usage time each device identifier has spent, on which feature and with which AI provider (see "Device identifier"). Groq is a US company with servers outside Israel; we can't tell you here how long it keeps the text. The conversation is not kept on the phone after it ends.

Avoiding it: these features are optional, and you can decline in the notice before the first use. At present the app has no single switch that turns them all off: the "הצגת AI" switch in Settings hides the AI conversation from the menu but does not block word guess or an AI item inside a set. If you don't want the text sent, don't use them.

The iPhone app contains a developer test screen, locked by a code, that holds a live voice conversation with OpenAI and sends it the sound itself. It is not part of normal use and doesn't open without the code.

Sharing with a therapist

Sharing is off until you start it, and you can stop it at any moment. It starts with the "התחלת שיתוף" (start sharing) button, and also when you turn on a second screen, when you scan a screen's connection code, and on iPhone also when you tap a notification of a meeting with your therapist. It goes through our sync server. Voice recordings are never sent by sharing, on either platform.

Sent when you start sharing, before you approve any watcher: the room code, the display name you chose, the device identifier, and the list of screens you already connected (their identifiers). From then on your display name and room code appear in the list of people sharing that therapists with access to the viewing page can see (see "Who can see it"). The practice itself (the text, the loudness trace, the words, the scores, the history, the goals and the thinking figures) waits for you to approve a watcher.

On iPhone and Android round summaries, goals and thinking figures are sent to the server and stored there only after you approve a watcher. Builds made before November 17, 2026 behaved differently: the goals and thinking figures were sent as soon as sharing started, and on iPhone so was a summary of every round finished while sharing was on (exercise name, scores, measurements and the recognised words), even without an approved watcher.

A watcher is approved once per browser or device. A screen or therapist you approved is remembered on the phone and let in on later visits without asking, until you remove it ("שכח את המסכים שאושרו" on iPhone, "לשכוח את כל המאושרים" on Android, both meaning forget the approved screens, or removing a connected screen). You can also switch the question off altogether ("אל תשאל לפני שמישהו מתחיל לצפות" or "אל תשאל בכל פעם" on iPhone, "בלי שאלת אישור למסכים חדשים" on Android; off by default): then anyone who enters the code sees the practice. And a screen you open yourself (with the second-screen button or by scanning a connection code, and on iPhone also from the link or QR code the app shows) gets in without a question: the next watcher who joins within three minutes is approved automatically, and remembered like any approved watcher.

Sent after you approve a watcher (a therapist, or your own second screen): live, the exercise text (including sentences you wrote), the loudness trace, the recognised words, scores and feedback, thresholds and measurement settings, the list of exercises in the set, your self-rating, and in a free conversation its transcript including the AI's replies. The phone also sends up to 200 round summaries from your history, including rounds practised while sharing was off, your goals with their starting point and history, and the last eight weeks of "speaking while thinking" figures.

Stored on the server: the round summaries (up to 200 per room code), the goals and their history, and the thinking figures are kept on our server so that the therapist can look at them when you are not connected. They are held in the server's memory only and deleted 30 days after the room's last update, or sooner: when the server restarts (which happens from time to time), or when the storage fills up (then the rooms updated longest ago are deleted first). There is not yet a button in the app that deletes them. Stopping sharing, removing a therapist or making a new code ("קוד חדש") does not delete what was already stored.

Who can see it: whoever you approved, live; a therapist you connected, also the stored summaries, also when you are not connected; and we, as the server's operator. Therapists we have given access to the viewing page can see a list of who is sharing, and also of who stopped sharing in the last half hour: display name, room code, and, once you have approved a watcher, the current exercise name (for an exercise you wrote yourself, the sentence itself). The display name need not be a full name. The server's operating logs do not include the room code or the display name: a room appears in them as an internal tag from which the code cannot be recovered. When the AI is used, the first eight characters of the device identifier are written to them. The logs are kept by the hosting provider for a limited time.

The room code is a key. The app has no accounts, so the 8-digit room code is what protects the data on the server: the room code alone is enough to read the history stored on the server (the round summaries, the goals and the thinking figures) without you being asked to approve anything. Guard it like a password and give it only to your therapist.

Remote control is off by default. If you turn on "אפשר למטפל לשלוט מרחוק" (on iPhone) or "לאפשר למטפל/ת לשלוט בתרגול (הבא, קודם, ספים, גרף, סטים)" (on Android), both meaning let the therapist control the practice, an approved watcher can change the exercise, change thresholds, send a set or a sentence, and start a round, which turns on the microphone and speech recognition while the practice screen is open.

The therapist's notes and the patient names the therapist writes down stay in the therapist's browser or phone and are not sent to us. When a therapist watches from an iPhone, that phone keeps the patient's name, the room code and the notes for each meeting, and they are included in that phone's backup (iCloud or a computer).

Device identifier, launch check-in and crash reports

The app uses a device identifier: a string derived (by SHA-256) from the identifier the system gives our apps on iPhone, or from a random number the app makes up on Android. It is not your phone number, not your Apple or Google account, and not an advertising identifier. It is used to meter the AI allowance and to identify the device in sharing. Because it is sent to the same server together with your display name when you share, it should not be thought of as anonymous.

On iPhone the app sends our server, at every launch and every return to it – only after you have accepted the notice at first launch: the device identifier, the app and system versions, and the "שם המכשיר" (device name) if you typed one in Settings (optional). If the app crashed, it sends the reason and code location of the crash at the next launch (after the notice is accepted). Builds made before 18 November 2026 sent this before the notice was accepted, and included the install dates. There is currently no switch to turn this off. On Android there is no launch check-in and there are no crash reports.

The server keeps, per device identifier: the versions, the first and last check-in times, the name you typed, crash reports, how much AI usage time was spent (in total, per feature and per AI provider), and whether we blocked the device or wrote a note about it. This is kept in the server's database until we delete it; we have not yet set a retention period.

"דיווח על בעיה" (report a problem) is a file you create and send yourself through the share sheet; it contains settings and recent scores. The name of an exercise you wrote yourself is the sentence itself, so it may appear in the file.

Where data is processed

Our server is hosted by Render, outside Israel. Microsoft, Apple, Google and Groq process what is sent to them on their own servers outside Israel, under their own terms. Everything is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Children

The app accepts ages from 3 and has games suited to children. For a child under 18, a parent or guardian, or the therapist together with the parent, installs the app, decides which features to turn on and agrees to its use. The app itself does not currently ask for a parent's approval and does not switch features off by age. For children we recommend choosing the voice built into the device, not using the AI conversation and word guess, and sharing only with the therapist.

Stopping and deleting

Stop sharing: "הפסק שיתוף" (on iPhone) or "הפסקת השיתוף" (on Android) on the sharing screen; watchers stop seeing at once. You can also remove a connected therapist or screen, forget the approved screens, and make a new code ("קוד חדש").

Delete from the phone: "מחיקת כל נתוני התרגול" on the results screen deletes scores and practice history only. Goals, sentences and recordings, the voice profile, age and sex remain; deleting the app deletes everything on the phone (on iPhone, except what is in a backup).

Delete from the server, see your data or correct it: write to support@spiraapps.com with your room code and, if you can, your device name. We will delete the sharing data, the device row, the crash reports and the usage figures linked to you. We cannot delete for you what was already sent to Microsoft, Apple, Google or Groq.

Changes

The app is in a trial stage and its behaviour changes. When something written here changes, we will update this page and the date at its top.
