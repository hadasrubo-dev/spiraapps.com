SwingSlam Privacy Policy

SwingSlam · Support · Play safely

Last updated: November 16, 2026

SwingSlam is a motion game for iPhone with Apple Watch, and for Android with Wear OS. This policy explains what happens to your data when you play. There is no account, no advertising and no tracking across apps or sites, and nothing is sold.

Motion

The watch or phone reads its own motion sensors to detect your swings. The sensor readings are processed on the device and summarised into a swing (its direction and strength); only that reaches the game. The last seconds of raw motion are kept in memory on the device for the Report described below, and are discarded otherwise.

Health (iPhone and Apple Watch)

SwingSlam's games are active workouts: while you play, the watch runs a workout session that tracks your movement. By default nothing is written to Apple Health. If you turn on Save to Apple Health, a play session is saved to Health as one workout with each game inside it (its kind, time, active energy and heart rate), and the app reads the heart rate and energy the watch measured during the game to show them to you afterwards and in its statistics. Health data stays on your devices and in your Apple Health; it is never sent to us or to anyone else, and is never used for advertising. You can turn the setting off at any time in Watch setup, and remove the workouts in the Health app.

Usage data

To know which games are played and whether the app works, the app sends to our server, at launch and after a finished game: a random identifier the app made up and keeps (not tied to your Apple ID, Google account or hardware), the app and system version, the game played, its duration, score and level, the playing hand, whether a motion controller (the watch or the phone in your hand) or the finger played, counts and average speeds of swings, and the player name you typed in the app, if you typed one, sent together with that identifier. The name is optional and needn't be your real name: a nickname, your phone's model or any text that helps players tell each other apart in 2- and 4-player games will do; it is not checked against anything. If the app crashes, it sends the crash's reason and stack. We don't ask for your real name, and send no location, no contacts, no health data, no motion recordings. The server runs on Render in Frankfurt (EU).

These statistics are stored in our database (hosted by Neon in Frankfurt, EU) and used only to see which games are played, where players stop and whether the app works, so we can improve it. They are not shared or sold. The identifier is a random number made up by the app, not a code derived from your device or account, so it cannot be traced back to either.

Turning it off: Setup → "Send anonymous play statistics" (on by default). With it off, the app sends no game summaries, and neither your player name nor your playing hand with its launch check-in. The check-in itself stays (the random identifier, the app and system version): it is how the app learns about notices such as a required update. In 2-player games your name still goes to the other player through the room, as described below.

Like any internet server, ours sees your device's IP address when the app connects; it is used only to limit abuse (too many requests from one address) and is not stored by us, though our hosting provider may keep it briefly in its own logs.

Two players and a second screen

Playing with another phone, or showing the game on a TV or tablet, goes through a short-lived room on our server or directly over your Wi‑Fi. What passes through is the game's moves and scores and the nickname you typed (it is shown to the other player). Rooms are kept in memory only and are gone half an hour after the players leave. Nicknames others typed are shown here with rude words filtered out. To find a phone close by, the app uses local networking (Bonjour) and, on request, the camera to scan the other phone's game code; the camera is used only while the scanner is open and no picture is kept.

Showing the game in a browser (tv.swingslam.com): the phone sends its picture straight to that browser over your Wi‑Fi, protected by a key in the address. Our server only hands the phone's local Wi‑Fi address to the browser that showed the code, once, and forgets it within 5 minutes; the picture never goes through the server. To send its picture, the app records its own screen (no microphone) only while a browser is watching.

Pictures (Hammer)

You can choose up to three of your own pictures for the Hammer game. Only the pictures you pick reach the app; they are stored on the device and never sent to us. They appear on another screen only if you show the game on one (the picture over your Wi‑Fi or in a browser, as above).

Reports you send

In test builds, the Report button writes a diagnostic file with the app's log (and the previous session's log, if the app was closed since), settings and the last 90 seconds of motion from the watch or the phone, and opens the share sheet. Nothing is sent unless you share it yourself (for example to support@swingslam.com). We use what you send only to fix the problem you reported.

Purchases

SwingSlam Full is sold through the App Store (on Android every game is free). Payment is handled entirely by Apple; we never see your payment details. The app only asks the store whether the purchase was made on your account, so it can be restored on your devices.

Data stored on your device

Settings, best scores, swing statistics and unlocked content are stored on the device, and removed when you uninstall the app.

Children

SwingSlam is not directed at children under 13. We don't ask anyone for personal information; the only thing a player could type about themselves is the optional player name. If a child typed a real name there, change it in the app, or write to us and we will delete it from our server.
